🇬🇭 FDA Guidelines

Food Safety & Legal FDA Standards

Ghana's Food and Drugs Authority (FDA) regulates commercial food preparation to protect the community under the Public Health Act 2012 (Act 851).

  • Food Handler Screening: All YenDidii hosts are strongly encouraged to obtain a Food Handler's Certificate from their municipal or metropolitan health office.
  • No Expired Ingredients: Never prepare or list dishes using expired ingredients. Always inspect labels before cooking.
  • Reporting Illness: If you show symptoms of foodborne illnesses, skin infections, or diarrhea, immediately pause listings and seek medical checkups.
  • Potable Water Sourcing: Always use clean, boiled, or sachet water for food preparation and handwashing. Do not use untreated well or borehole water.
🧼 Clean Kitchen

Personal & Kitchen Hygiene

Maintaining high standards of personal and station hygiene prevents cross-contamination and illness.

  • 20-Second Rule: Wash hands with clean soap and warm running water for at least 20 seconds before starting. Repeat after handling raw meat, trash, or money.
  • Host's Attire: Wear a clean kitchen apron and use a hairnet or scarf to prevent hair from falling into dishes.
  • Knife & Surface Hygiene: Wash knives, bowls, and tables with soap and clean water immediately after cutting or preparing ingredients.
  • No Jewellery: Remove watches, rings, and bracelets before cooking as they trap bacteria. Keep fingernails short and unpolished.
❄️ Danger Zone

Safe Storage & Danger Zone

Bacteria multiply exponentially at room temperature. Proper temperature checks keep food fresh.

  • The Danger Zone (5°C to 60°C): Keep food above 60°C or below 5°C. Never leave cooked rice, stews, or soups at room temperature for more than 2 hours.
  • Reheating: Reheat leftovers thoroughly until steaming hot (above 75°C) before serving. Never reheat a dish more than once.
  • Raw & Cooked Separation: Keep raw meats/poultry on the bottom shelves of the refrigerator to avoid raw juices dripping onto ready-to-eat foods.
  • Cutting Board Separation: Use separate cutting boards (plastic for raw poultry/meats, wood for vegetables) to avoid bacterial transmission.
🤝 Akwaaba Warmth

Hosting Guests & Hospitality

YenDidii hosts are ambassadors of traditional Ghanaian hospitality. Dine-In guests should experience a clean, welcoming environment.

  • The Akwaaba Welcome: Receive guests with a friendly smile, a clean seating space, and a glass of clean water.
  • Allergy Disclosures: Explicitly mention if you use peanuts (groundnuts), seafood, or common dairy allergens in your meals.
  • Spice Levels: Warn guests if your dish is highly spiced. Offer alternatives or keep extra rice/water nearby.
  • Reviews: Encourage guests to rate your session in the app. Positive reviews help you stand out on the map.
